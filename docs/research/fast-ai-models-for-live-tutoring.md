# Haiku 5.5 beats Inkling for live tutoring

For a live, camera-based math tutor in late November 2026, **Claude Haiku 5.5 is the best overall model, and Inkling 2 Small is not the right choice for the live conversation**. In Thinking Machines' own comparison table, Haiku 5.5 scores higher on the Artificial Analysis (AA) intelligence index (36.5 vs 30.6). On AA's speed tests it also streams about twice as fast (217–254 vs 111 tokens per second), it costs less ($0.10/$0.50 vs $0.30/$1.00 per million input/output tokens), and it can switch thinking off completely. Inkling 2 Small is excellent at reading documents (OmniDocBench 94.6, the best score in its table), but it has no audio input, takes 45 s to its first answer word at default settings, is wordy by its makers' own account, and needs about 500 GB of GPU memory, so it cannot run on your Mac. The **fastest pick is Qwen3.8-27B on Cerebras** (about 1,850 tokens per second, vendor figure): at that speed, the "check with thinking" that takes you ~44 s today should take a few seconds (my estimate). The **best fully local pick is qwen3.5:35b** (35B total parameters, only 3B active per token) in place of qwen3.5:9b: it beats the 9B on every math and vision score Qwen reports and should also generate faster. One result changes how to think about speed: your local hints (0.4 s to first token) already start faster than the fastest cloud models (about 1.2–1.3 s median on OpenRouter), so the cloud buys you better answers and much faster thinking, not a faster first word. No independent handwriting-math test covers any of these new models, so a 20–30-photo test on real student work should make the final decision.

## Inkling 2 Small reads pages well but answers too slowly to talk

Thinking Machines released **Inkling 2 Small on November 5, 2026** and published its weights on November 12. It is a 594B-parameter mixture-of-experts (MoE) model with 17B parameters active per token, a 1M-token context and an Apache 2.0 license ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small)). It takes text and images and returns text. The launch post says plainly that **"this model does not support audio"** ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)). Its strength is vision on documents. In the vendor's own comparison it leads on OmniDocBench and is close on most other rows, but it trails Claude Haiku 5.5 on the overall index and Gemini 3.8 Flash on general vision:

| Benchmark (vendor table) | Inkling 2 Small | Gemini 3.8 Flash | Claude Haiku 5.5 | GPT-6 Luna |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AA Intelligence Index v5.0 | 30.6 | 30.8 | **36.5** | 27.9 |
| OmniDocBench v1.6 (document reading) | **94.6** | 89.7 | 92.6 | 92.3 |
| MMMU Pro (visual reasoning) | 81.6 | **85.6** | 79.1 | 79.7 |
| HLE, text only | 44.1 | **47.8** | 44.4 | 38.5 |
| ARC-AGI-2 | 81.7 | **89.2** | 61.3 | 59.3 |

All figures above are vendor-reported ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small)). Thinking Machines published **no AIME, HMMT or MATH score** for this model, so its school-math ability is probably fine but not verified ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)). AA's independent index gives it **31, ranked 5th of 33** comparable open-weight models ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/inkling-2-small)).

The problem for a tutor is speed and style. AA measured **111 output tokens/s (rank 24 of 30) and 45.2 s to the first answer word**, against a peer median of 21.3 s. It is also wordy: it used 154M output tokens on AA's test set, against a peer median of 104M ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/inkling-2-small)). Thinking Machines itself says the model is "not the most succinct communicator", organizes chat answers poorly and **overuses slashes** ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)). Its model card warns of **degraded performance in long multi-turn conversations** ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small)). A spoken tutor needs the opposite: short, clean answers across many turns. Lower effort settings exist, but nobody has published latency at those settings, so that is the main open question. Price is not a problem: $0.30/$1.00 per million tokens on Thinking Machines' API and as low as $0.15/$0.50 through OpenRouter ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/inkling-2-small); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/thinkingmachines/inkling-2-small)). Running it locally is impossible: even the 4-bit NVFP4 version needs **at least 500 GB** of GPU memory ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small)).

The two other Inkling models fit different niches. **Inkling Small** (July 30, 2026; 276B total, 12B active) is the only family member that accepts audio. One request can carry the webcam photo plus a 16 kHz WAV of the spoken question as an `input_audio` part ([Tinker cookbook](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/cookbook/inkling/audio/)). Its vendor math scores are strong (AIME 2026 95.5%, HMMT Feb 2026 90.2%) ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small)), and AA ranks it the most concise model it tests. But AA now marks it **deprecated**, scores it only 10–13 on the current index (rank 30 of 33), and measured 24.5 s to the first answer word at high effort ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/inkling-small)). **Inkling 2 Mini**, released November 20, 2026, is the only one that fits your Mac. It is a 26.4B MoE model of about 20 GB that Ollama supports on Macs with 32 GB or more, with an AA index of 20 (equal to Qwen3.8-27B) and OmniDocBench 92.8 ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)). It is one day old, has no measured Mac speed, does not mention audio, and its makers report weaker multi-turn chat (MultiChallenge 50.0%). The verdict: **do not build the live tutor on any Inkling model today**. Inkling 2 Small is worth a test as a cheap second reader of worksheet photos at low effort, and Inkling 2 Mini is worth one local trial.

## Thinking time, not model size, decides how long students wait

Almost every strong model in 2026 "thinks" (writes hidden reasoning) before it answers, and this hidden text must be generated before the first visible word appears. AA's "time to first answer token" includes the thinking, so it shows the cost clearly. **Claude Haiku 5.5 takes 6.0 s at low effort, 9.2 s at medium, 20.5 s at high and 176 s at max** ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-haiku-5-5)). Gemini 3.8 Flash goes from 5.1 s at low to 28.1 s at high ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/releases/gemini-3-8-flash)), and GPT-6 Luna reaches 2.55 s at low ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-6-luna-low/providers)). AA uses hard benchmark questions, so a school exercise needs less thinking and real turns will be faster. The pattern, however, matches your own result: 2.7 s without thinking, 44 s with it.

Two rules follow. First, **for hints and spoken questions, use models that can switch thinking fully off**. GPT-6 Luna can (`reasoning_effort: none`), Claude Haiku 5.5 can (`thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`, allowed at effort high or below), and the Qwen models can (`enable_thinking: False`, or `reasoning_effort: none` on Groq) ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna); [Claude effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort); [HF Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B); [Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/model/qwen/qwen3.8-27b)). Gemini 3.8 Flash and the Gemini 4 models cannot go below `low`, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 can only postpone thinking (`between_tools`) ([Gemini thinking docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/thinking); [Claude effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)). Second, **when you do need thinking (checking work, solving step by step), output speed matters more than anything else**, because thousands of thinking tokens must stream before the answer. The table estimates how long the thinking in your 44 s check would take elsewhere. I assume your Mac generates qwen3.5:9b at 40–80 tokens/s (community runs show 39–50 tokens/s on an M4 Pro) ([llmcheck](https://llmcheck.net/benchmarks)), so the check produced roughly 2,000–3,500 thinking tokens; I use 2,500.

| Where the thinking runs | Output speed (tokens/s) | Time for ~2,500 thinking tokens (my estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| qwen3.5:9b on your Mac | ~40–80 | 30–60 s (you measured 44 s) |
| Inkling 2 Small, Thinking Machines API | 111 | ~23 s |
| Gemini 4 Hydrogen | 165–170 | ~15 s |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | 217–254 | ~10–12 s |
| Qwen3.8-27B on Groq | ~450 (vendor) | ~6 s |
| Qwen3.8-27B on Cerebras | ~1,850 (vendor) | ~1.4 s |
| gpt-oss-120b on Cerebras (text only) | ~3,000 (vendor) | ~0.8 s |

Speeds come from AA for Inkling 2 Small and Haiku 5.5 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/inkling-2-small); [AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-haiku-5-5)), from AA data reported by Valyu for Hydrogen ([Valyu](https://www.valyu.ai/blogs/gemini-4-neon-vs-carbon-vs-hydrogen-vs-argon)), and from vendor docs for Groq and Cerebras ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/models); [Cerebras](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/models/overview)). Real times also include network delay, and models think for different lengths. Inkling 2 Small is verbose, and Qwen3.8-27B defaults to `xhigh` effort, which used about 110K output tokens per AA task, so set it to `low` ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models?weights=open)). Even with these caveats, **ultra-fast hosts turn a 44 s thinking check into a wait of a few seconds**.

For short answers with no thinking, the picture flips. Your local hints reach the first token in 0.4 s. In the cloud, OpenRouter's median latency on real traffic is **1.24 s for GPT-6 Luna, 1.30 s for Haiku 5.5 and 2.79 s for Gemini 3.8 Flash** ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/compare/openai/gpt-5.6-luna/openai/gpt-6-luna); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/compare/anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5/google/gemini-3.8-flash)). AA measured Cerebras' gpt-oss-120b at 0.69 s at low effort ([AA Cerebras](https://artificialanalysis.ai/providers/cerebras)). A cloud hint therefore starts about one second later than your local one, but it comes from a much stronger model and finishes quickly at 150–250 tokens/s. A simple trick hides that second: show or say a short, fixed acknowledgement ("Let me look at step 3…") as soon as the request starts.

**Claude Haiku 5.5** (released October 7, 2026; no retirement before October 7, 2027) is the balanced choice ([Claude Haiku 5.5 overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/haiku-5-5/overview)). It has the highest index of the cheap fast models, the fastest stream, a 1.3 s median latency and a full thinking-off switch. Anthropic's own advice is to use effort `low` for chat, where the model "can skip thinking entirely for simpler problems" ([Claude effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)). It cannot hear audio, so it needs a separate speech-to-text step. **GPT-6 Luna** costs the same and starts slightly sooner, but scores lower (28 vs 36), and on Chat Completions it supports function calling only with reasoning set to `none` ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna)). It is a solid backup. OpenAI's small models are now named Luna (smallest), Terra, Sol and Astra; GPT-6.1 Terra is much smarter (index 51) at $1/$5, but took 168 s to its first answer word at max effort ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/openai/api-pricing); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)).

**Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (released November 12, 2026) is the smartest fast model. At medium effort (index 41) it already beats Haiku 5.5 at max (36), it streams at 165–170 tokens/s, it accepts audio and images in one request, and it has a free tier ([Valyu](https://www.valyu.ai/blogs/gemini-4-neon-vs-carbon-vs-hydrogen-vs-argon); [Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Its weaknesses for live turns: thinking cannot be switched off, its default `high` effort took 20 s to the first answer word on AA, and free-tier data may be used to improve Google's products, which matters when the photos show children's homework ([Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). That makes it a good "solve step by step" or second-opinion model, not the always-on voice. The other Gemini 4 models (Neon, Carbon, Argon) score 50–61 but take 40–73 s to the first answer word at high effort ([Valyu](https://www.valyu.ai/blogs/gemini-4-neon-vs-carbon-vs-hydrogen-vs-argon)).

The rest of the field is weaker for this job. **Gemini 3.8 Flash** scores below Haiku 5.5 (31 vs 36.5), streams at only 63 tokens/s on OpenRouter, cost $7.77 per AA task because of heavy thinking, and its price doubles on January 1, 2027 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/releases/gemini-3-8-flash); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/compare/anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5/google/gemini-3.8-flash); [Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Its one standout is the best MMMU Pro score (85.6) in Thinking Machines' table. **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite** is fast but scores only 12. **Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol** are much smarter (indexes 55–59) but take 82–357 s to the first answer word at max effort ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). **xAI has no usable fast option.** Grok 4.7 scores 35 but needed 62.9 s at xhigh, Grok 4.7 Fast is reported to be unavailable on the public API, and Grok 4.1 Fast was retired on May 15, 2026 ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/grok-api-pricing)). Mistral's Ministral 3 models (1.5–2.3 s) and Amazon Nova Micro (1.3 s) start fast, but I found no intelligence-index or math evidence for them ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)).

For a voice-to-voice design, **Gemini 3.8 Live** (`gemini-3.8-live`) streams audio in and out. It measured about 861 ms voice-to-voice on the Coval benchmark, as reported by Cartesia, a company that sells parts for the alternative design ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/speech-to-speech-vs-cascaded)). It is also cheap: $3/$12 per million audio tokens, about $0.005 per minute heard and $0.018 per minute spoken, with image and video input priced too and a free tier ([Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). OpenAI's **gpt-realtime-2.1** takes audio and images and can reason, but its audio costs about ten times more ($32/$64 per million tokens) ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-realtime-2.1.md)). Nobody has measured how well either one checks math inside a live session, so use them for a natural-sounding demo, not as the only brain.

Handwriting is the largest unknown. **No independent handwriting or handwritten-math benchmark covers any November 2026 model.** Older tests favor Gemini: Gemini 3 Pro led a cursive-handwriting benchmark ([LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/insights/best-ai-for-handwriting-recognition)), and an EMNLP 2026 study found Gemini 2.5 Flash the most faithful transcriber of handwritten math ([EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.476.pdf)). The same study found that vision models **"fix" students' mistakes while transcribing in 42–66% of cases, and larger models in a family do it more often** ([EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.476.pdf)). For a work checker this is dangerous: the model may approve a correct line that the student never wrote. Your small glm-ocr model, which only transcribes, is therefore worth keeping.

Cerebras serves Qwen3.8-27B at about 1,850 tokens/s and gpt-oss-120b at about 3,000 tokens/s. OpenRouter lists Cerebras' Qwen3.8-27B at $0.99/$1.49 ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/qwen/qwen3.8-27b)), and new Cerebras accounts get a $5 credit that expires after 30 days ([Cerebras pricing](https://www.cerebras.ai/pricing)). Prefer Qwen3.8-27B to gpt-oss-120b: AA's index gives it 20 against 4 for gpt-oss-120b, and gpt-oss cannot see images ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models?weights=open)). Qwen3.8-27B ranks below Haiku 5.5 on AA's index (20 vs 36), but it is well above your current 9B on Qwen's own vision-math score (MathVision 90.0 vs 78.9). The biggest open models (Kimi K3.1 Pro, index 46; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 33) are smarter, but they need 45–154 s at max effort and are not on these fast hosts ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models?weights=open)).

## Speech adds less than a second if the pipeline is built well

For speech-to-text on the Mac, **NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2** is the fastest accurate choice. In one test on an M4, a short question took **0.19 s** through FluidAudio (Core ML) and 0.50 s through parakeet-mlx, against 1.0–1.2 s for Whisper large-v3-turbo; note that this test dates from 2025 ([LocalAIMaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/best-speech-to-text-mac)). It is also more accurate: a 6.93% word error rate on the November 2026 Open ASR leaderboard, against 8.75% for Whisper turbo ([ThinkScribe](https://thinkscribe.ai/en/blog/open-speech-recognition-models-2026)). Parakeet v2 is English-only. Version 3 covers 25 European languages and Whisper turbo about 99, which matters if your students do not always ask in English ([ThinkScribe](https://thinkscribe.ai/en/blog/open-speech-recognition-models-2026)). **Moonshine v2** (English only) responds in 50–258 ms on an M3 CPU and can run in the browser or in Node.js ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241v1); [browser-whisper](https://github.com/tanpreetjolly/browser-whisper)). If you prefer an API, **OpenAI gpt-transcribe** costs $0.0045 per minute, scores 3.3% on AA's word-error test and accepts a `keywords` list, so you can feed it terms from the exercise the OCR just read. ElevenLabs Scribe v2 scores 2.2%, and Groq's hosted Whisper turbo costs about $0.67 per 1,000 minutes ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/openai-gpt-transcribe-stt-model-comparison-2026)). No source tested spoken math such as "x squared" or "three over four", so test this yourself.

Deciding when the student has finished speaking can cost more time than the model. **Silero VAD v6** (MIT license) is the standard voice-activity detector and runs in the browser through the `vad-web` package ([Silero](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/discussions/678); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/voice-ai-agent-browser/)). TEN VAD claims faster end detection, but its license adds a non-compete clause ([VoxRT](https://voxrt.com/vad-comparison)). Students pause while thinking, so a very short silence timer cuts them off and a long one feels slow. The practical options are about 0.7–1.0 s of silence, a semantic turn detector such as Pipecat Smart Turn v3 ([audioflow issue](https://github.com/flowcopilot/audioflow/issues/10)), or a simple push-to-talk button. Deepgram Flux ($0.0065 per minute) does end-of-turn detection on the server, with a vendor-claimed latency under 300 ms ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/openai-gpt-transcribe-stt-model-comparison-2026); [Future AGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/speech-to-text-apis-in-2026-benchmarks-pricing-developer-s-decision-guide/)).

For text-to-speech, **ElevenLabs Flash v2.5** has the most consistent independent measurements: about 188–198 ms to first audio on Coval. Inworld TTS-2 Flash measured about 81 ms in one snapshot, while Cartesia Sonic measured 269–440 ms despite a 40–90 ms vendor claim ([Coval](https://www.coval.ai/blog/time-to-first-audio-ttfa-benchmark/)). **Kokoro** is the main free local option, with under 1 s to first audio on an M3 Pro CPU ([ForaSoft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/streaming-tts-kokoro-elevenlabs-turbo-openai-tts)); the browser's built-in speech and macOS `say` need no setup at all. Because no voice reads LaTeX well, ask the tutor model for a separate "spoken" version of each answer ("x squared", not "x^2"). A pipeline like this (detector, speech-to-text, model, speech) reached a median of about 958 ms voice-to-voice in one 2026 study, close to Gemini Live's 861 ms ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/speech-to-speech-vs-cascaded)), and it keeps a visible transcript that the student can check.

## The picks, and a concrete stack for your app

**Best overall: Claude Haiku 5.5.** It gives the best mix of quality (highest index among cheap fast models), speed (fastest stream, about 1.3 s median latency, thinking can be off) and price (well under one cent per turn). Use thinking off for hints and spoken questions, effort `low` for checks and `medium` for full solutions. Send hints and checks as separate requests, because with thinking disabled the effort level cannot change within one conversation ([Claude effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)). **Fastest: Qwen3.8-27B on Cerebras**, fed with your glm-ocr text, for any turn that needs thinking; use Groq's version when you want the model to see the photo itself. **Best fully local: qwen3.5:35b** as the everyday tutor, with qwen3.8:27b at low effort for hard checks, plus glm-ocr, Parakeet and Kokoro. Gemini 4 Hydrogen is the step-up for difficult problems and second opinions. Gemini 3.8 Live is the choice if a voice-to-voice demo matters more than control.

My latency estimate for a spoken question with the hybrid stack: 0.7–1.0 s silence wait, 0.2–0.5 s speech-to-text, about 1.3 s to the model's first token and about 0.2 s to first audio. That is roughly **2.4–3.0 s from the end of speech to the first spoken word**, or about 1.7–2.0 s with push-to-talk. Thinking checks on Cerebras should add a few seconds, not the 40+ seconds they take locally today.

Run one test before you commit. Your 7/7 result shows the current setup works, but seven items cannot separate good models from great ones. Take 20–30 webcam photos of real handwritten work, put a deliberate mistake in half of them, and run Haiku 5.5, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Qwen3.8-27B on Cerebras or Groq, and local qwen3.5:35b. Record three things for each: whether the transcription keeps the student's mistake, whether the model finds the mistake, and the time to the first word and to the end of the answer.

## Conclusion

For a live tutor, **where a model thinks and how much it thinks matter more than which model it is**. The same 2,500 thinking tokens take about 45 seconds on your laptop and about one second on Cerebras, and every major model now has an effort setting that changes latency by ten times or more. Benchmark headlines hide this: Inkling 2 Small tops a document-reading table, yet its makers say it is less optimized for chat, and short, clear chat is exactly what a spoken tutor needs. A second point is less obvious: reading student work and solving math are different jobs. Bigger vision models are more likely to "correct" a student's mistake while transcribing it, so the tiny OCR model in your current stack protects the checker rather than slowing it down.

This market also changes every week. Gemini 4 Hydrogen is nine days old, Inkling 2 Mini is one day old, and Gemini 3.8 Flash's price doubles in six weeks. Put the model choice behind one setting in your code (OpenRouter alone lists Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Inkling 2 Small and Qwen3.8-27B), keep your test photos, and re-run the test whenever a new fast model appears.
